# Mul

> "See, the trick is to break their will. Not too much, mind you. Nobody wants to watch a docile gladiator, and muls are too expensive to waste as labor slaves. But, you don't want them trying to escape every other day. Would you like to tell the arena crowd that their favorite champion will not be appearing in today's match because he died trying to escape your pens?"
> — Gaal, Urikite arena trainer

## Overview

Born from unlikely dwarven and human parentage, muls combine human height and adaptability with dwarven musculature and resilience. Unique traits include robust metabolism and almost inexhaustible capacity for work. Disadvantages: sterility and social repercussions of being bred for slavery. Humans and dwarves are not typically attracted to each other — muls are common because slave-sellers force-breed for profit. Breeding is lucrative but often lethal to mother and baby. Conception is difficult, often taking months. Gestation is twelve months with high fatalities. Anxious overseers often cut muls from dying mothers.

## Personality

Gladiators who perform well receive pampered treatment; muls receive more than most. Some mul gladiators accept slavery. Those who taste freedom fight for it. Stoic and dull to pain, muls are not easily intimidated by the lash. Masters loathe slaying or maiming a mul who repeatedly tries to escape, though those who help escape face torment to punish the mul without damaging valuable property. Once a mul escapes or earns freedom, slavery remains dominant — most are heavily tattooed with ownership, history, capabilities, and disciplinary marks. Even untattooed muls are marked as windfall for slavers who claim "retrieval" is cheaper than breeding pits.

## Physical description

Second only to half-giants in strength among common humanoids. Up to seven feet, upwards of 250 pounds, almost no fat on broad muscular frames. Universal characteristics: angular protrusive eye ridges, ears pointing sharply back against temples. Dark copper-colored skin; hairless bodies.

## Relations

Most laborer muls master slave-life conventions, learning through painful experience who can be trusted. They survive mistakes where other slave races are disposable — only the most foolish disobedient mul would be killed; most masters sell problem muls rather than kill them. Mastery of slave rules and tireless metabolism wins favor with masters and reputation among fellow slaves.

## Alignment

Tend toward neutrality on good/evil but run the gamut on law/chaos. Lawful muls adapt to slavery's indignities, playing the game for comforts as valued slaves; some organize rebellions. Chaotic muls push luck and value to the breaking point, defying authority with little fear of the lash.

## Lands

No lands of their own. Escaped muls sometimes band as outlaws with common ex-slave backgrounds. Almost all are born in merchant and noble slave pits of city-states — laborers, gladiators, or soldier-slaves. Few earn freedom; more escape to ex-slave tribes of the wastes.

## Magic

Dislike what they fear — wizards. Resent power from without with no apparent effort while mul power comes from pain and labor. Mul wizards are unheard of.

## Psionics

Most owners prevent schooling in the Way; formal training is rare. Those who receive it often excel in psychometabolic powers.

## Religion

As sterile hybrids, muls have no posterity to pass religion to. Some cities accept mul templars. Mul clerics tend toward elemental earth's strength.

## Language

Common (slave tongue). Favored muls in one city long enough sometimes learn the city language. Tireless metabolism lets muls integrate with peoples others could not — elves and thri-kreen.

## Names

Labor slaves: common slave names. Gladiators: striking exotic names — Draji names (e.g. Atlalak) popular for Draji violence reputation. Masters changing profession usually change names — bad form for a gladiator to keep a farmer's name; dangerous to give a laborer a gladiator's name.

## Adventurers

PC muls are assumed to have won freedom. Most freed gladiators use combat skills as soldiers or guards; some turn to crime. A few follow psionics, templar orders, or elemental priesthoods.

## Society

No racial history or separate culture. Sterile — cannot form family groups or clans. Vast majority born in slavery through breeding programs. Parents often resent the role and shun the child — lonely, hard existence; taskmaster's whip replaces family. Many muls never seek friendship; rough personalities with violent tendencies. Slave trade is very profitable; breeding continues. Traders profit as much from one mul as a dozen humans. Slaves have profession selected and extensive training. Gladiator muls often very successful. Highly successful gladiators receive large retinues of slaves — origin of "pampered like a mul."

## Roleplaying suggestions

Born to slave pens — no love or affection; the taskmaster's whip replaced parents. Life's problems that can be solved are solved by brute force. Bow to force when you see it, especially veiled force of wealth, power, and privilege. Noble and templar may not look strong but can kill with a word. Gruffness. Some muls never sought friends; others found friendship in arena partners or co-workers. Capable of affection, trust, friendship — but camaraderie is easier: warriors slap shoulders after victory or give lives in battle. You do not think of that as "friendship" — it just happens.

## Racial traits

- **+4 Strength, +2 Constitution, −2 Charisma:** Stronger than either parent race; born-to-be-slave status makes insecure in dealings with others.
- **Humanoid (dwarf).**
- **Medium:** No size bonuses or penalties.
- **Speed:** Base land speed 30 feet.
- **Darkvision** 30 feet (black and white only).
- **Tireless:** +4 racial bonus on checks for physical actions over time (running, swimming, holding breath, etc.); stacks with Endurance. Also applies to saves against weakness, fatigue, exhaustion, or enfeeblement from spells and magical effects.
- **Extended activity:** Up to 12 hours hard labor or forced marching without fatigue.
- **Dwarven blood:** For all race-related effects, considered a dwarf.
- **Nonlethal damage resistance 1/−:** Difficult to subdue; ignore minor bruises and scrapes other races feel.
- **Automatic language:** Common.
- **Bonus languages:** Dwarven, Elven, Gith, Giant.
- **Favored class:** Gladiator (does not count when determining multiclass XP penalty).
- **Level adjustment +1.**
